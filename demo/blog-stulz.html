ICT & Logistics 2019 — the STULZ story

October 2019

← Back to blog

At ICT & Logistics 2019 in the Netherlands, STULZ GROEP B.V. shared why it chose the A5 Print & Ship label with nShift Transsmart — to sharpen its dispatch process and take single-use plastic out of the box.

ICT & Logistics is one of the Netherlands' leading events for logistics and supply-chain technology, and it gave STULZ GROEP B.V. a fitting stage to talk about a change it had made to its shipping process. STULZ was looking for two things at once: a tidier, more reliable way to dispatch parcels, and a way to reduce the packaging waste that came with it.

The challenge

Like many operations, STULZ was printing a shipping label and a separate packing list, then combining them by hand and enclosing the packing list in a single-use plastic document pouch on the outside of each box. It worked, but it involved extra handling, extra materials, and more room for a mismatch between a label and its contents.

The solution — A5 Print & Ship with nShift Transsmart

STULZ adopted the A5 Print & Ship label, which merges the shipping label and packing list into a single duplex-printed document. The document is generated directly from nShift Transsmart, the delivery management software already coordinating the company's carriers and shipments, and printed on a standard duplex laser printer.

  • Shipping label and packing list combined into one duplex document
  • Generated from within nShift Transsmart
  • The single-use plastic document pouch removed entirely

By printing the packing list onto the back of the shipping label, STULZ no longer needed the plastic pouch to carry a loose sheet. That single change removed a piece of single-use plastic from every parcel — a meaningful reduction across the volumes a group like STULZ ships.

The A5 Print & Ship duplex label gave us a genuine process improvement. Merging the shipping label and packing list into one document simplified the bench, and let us remove the single-use plastic envelopes from our parcels for good.

The result

The packing process became simpler and steadier: one document to print and apply, with the label and its contents guaranteed to match because they came from the same sheet. Conny van Buuren of STULZ pointed to the combination of process improvement and reduced waste as the reason the solution made sense for the group.

STULZ's story at ICT & Logistics 2019 captured what Pinnpoint set out to build — a shipping document that is faster to handle and lighter on the planet, running on the software a team already has.
