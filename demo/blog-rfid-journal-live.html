Highlights from the show floor — and the conversations shaping RFID-enabled shipping documents.

RFID Journal Live is the RFID industry's flagship annual gathering, bringing together the people building and deploying RAIN RFID across retail, logistics and manufacturing. For Pinnpoint, it was a chance to talk about a slightly different way of thinking about RFID in the shipping process.

RFID in the document, not a separate tag

Much of the show-floor conversation still centred on tagging as its own step. Pinnpoint's angle was to embed the UHF inlay directly into the sheeted label or document a team already prints — so the tag arrives as part of the shipping paperwork rather than as an extra operation.

  • UHF inlays built into sheeted labels and shipping documents
  • Encoded and printed on RFID-capable duplex laser printers
  • Item-level visibility without a separate tagging station

The recurring theme in those conversations was practicality: teams want the visibility RFID promises, but not the friction of bolting a new process onto an already busy bench. Building the inlay into the existing document is one way to close that gap.
